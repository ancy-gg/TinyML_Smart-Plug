\chapter*{ABSTRACT}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{ABSTRACT}
This study developed a TinyML Smart Plug for complementary socket-level
protection against progressive fire-inducing electrical faults in 230V
residential circuits. The device was designed to address fault
conditions that may remain hazardous before conventional breaker
operation, specifically sustained overload, degraded socket-contact
heating, and load-limited series arc faults. The prototype integrated
voltage, current, and temperature sensing with an ESP32-S3 controller,
relay-based load isolation, local audio-visual alerts, Firebase logging,
and a Progressive Web App for monitoring. The system was tested through
sensor calibration and revalidation, controlled overload evaluation,
degraded-socket heating characterization, induced series-arc data
collection, waveform-feature extraction, manual labeling, and embedded
Random Forest TinyML deployment. Revalidation showed mean absolute
errors of 3.910V, 0.178A, and 1.02°C for the voltage, current, and
temperature channels, respectively. Overload testing confirmed
sustained-trip behavior, with the highest-stress case reaching 17.30A
mean current, 19.73A peak current, and relay disconnection after
61.382s. Heating tests showed faster temperature rise in the degraded
socket than in the normal socket under comparable loading. For series
arc detection, the embedded model achieved 97.50\% accuracy, 98.97\%
precision, 96.00\% recall, and 97.46\% F1-score across tested loads.
Overall, the study demonstrated the feasibility of combining calibrated
sensing, threshold-based protection, and on-device TinyML inference in a
single smart plug intended to complement, not replace, existing
residential electrical protection.

\textbf{Keywords:} TinyML, Smart Plug, Series Arc Fault Detection,
Socket Heating, Overload Protection
