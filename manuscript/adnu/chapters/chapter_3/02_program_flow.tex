\section{Program and Firmware Flow}
\label{sec:ch3-program-flow}

This section introduces the firmware flow that connects the measured hardware
signals to the protection and reporting behavior. The discussion follows the
same order used by the embedded program: initialization, repeated sampling and
feature computation, state evaluation, relay actuation, and cloud/PWA updates.

\subsection{Initialization and Sampling}
\label{sec:ch3-initialization-sampling}

The firmware follows a repeated acquisition, computation, inference, and
protection cycle. On startup, the ESP32-S3 initializes the sensors, relay
driver, OLED display, buzzer, Wi-Fi, Firebase interface, and local protection
state. In the main loop, it samples the voltage, thermistor, and current
waveform, applies calibration and filtering, computes thermal and waveform
features, updates the context classifier, evaluates the arc classifier, and
passes the resulting variables to the protection manager.

Figure~\ref{fig:ch3-program-flow} shows the operating sequence implemented by
the firmware.
\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{ch3/figure_3.3.png}
  \caption{Program flowchart of the TinyML Smart Plug firmware.}
  \label{fig:ch3-program-flow}
\end{figure}

The flowchart separates slow supervision from waveform-based inference. RMS
voltage, RMS current, and thermistor temperature are updated for ordinary
monitoring and deterministic protection. In parallel, the current waveform is
sampled at the higher ADC rate, conditioned, framed, and converted into
candidate waveform features. Usable feature frames update the load-context
classifier first; the accepted load-family code and confidence are then
appended to the arc-model input vector before Random Forest inference. The
result is not used as a single-frame trip command. It is passed to the
protection manager, where persistence counters, blanking intervals, and fault
latches determine whether the relay should remain energized, warn the user, or
disconnect the load. Another is that since the system targets fire-inducing 
faults, thermistor temperature is 
treated with the highest priority because it is the most direct indicator 
of socket heating. Arc-fault inference is evaluated next because waveform 
discontinuities and intermittent conduction can produce localized heating 
even before a sustained current burden is established. Overload protection 
is handled after these conditions because excessive current is treated as a 
time-dependent stress that requires persistence before relay action. Thus, 
the protection manager resolves the fault sequence by prioritizing 
temperature, then arc fault, and then overload.

\subsection{Protection-State Evaluation}
\label{sec:ch3-protection-state-evaluation}

Figure~\ref{fig:ch3-program-flow} shows that a relay trip can be caused by a
sustained overload, an estimated socket over-temperature, or an arc-fault
classification. The overload path issues a warning above 10.0A, but relay
disconnection is based on persistence above 14.5A. This persistence is
implemented as a score that increases every 10s while the
condition remains active and trips when the score reaches 6, equivalent to
approximately 60s of sustained overload. This makes the overload behavior
closer to inverse-time protection logic than to an instantaneous cutoff [22].

The socket-heating path compares the estimated socket temperature with thermal
warning and trip thresholds. The voltage path provides abnormal-supply
supervision and relay response where implemented, but it is not presented as a
replacement for a surge protective device, AVR, GFCI, AFCI, branch-circuit
breaker, or certified wiring protection. The firmware also latches fault
states. After relay disconnection, the event is recorded locally and uploaded
when the network is available. The plug remains in the fault state until the
user clears the fault through the device or application workflow. This
prevents a hazardous load from being automatically re-energized after a
transient communication or reset event.

\subsection{Timing Constants}
\label{sec:ch3-timing-constants}

The sampling, protection, and reporting constants used by the firmware are
listed in Table~\ref{tab:ch3-firmware-constants}. These values were taken from
the firmware configuration file and the current-feature implementation, so
they describe the deployed firmware behavior rather than nominal design
wishes.

    Load-context acquisition window & 5s & Time allowed for stable load-family estimation before latching context \\
    Overload warning threshold & 10.0A & Local warning level before sustained overload trip scoring \\

    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}

Table~\ref{tab:ch3-firmware-constants} explains why the current path is
handled separately from slow telemetry. Arc-fault detection requires waveform
frames with millisecond-scale timing, while Firebase updates and PWA displays
operate at a slower human-monitoring cadence. Consequently, relay protection
does not wait for Firebase or the PWA; the remote interface records,
visualizes, and sends basic control fields, while the local firmware evaluates
the safety states on the device.
