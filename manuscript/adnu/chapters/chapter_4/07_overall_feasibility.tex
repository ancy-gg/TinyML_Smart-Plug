\section{Prototype Feasibility Assessment}
\label{sec:ch4-overall-feasibility}

Taken together, the overload, socket-heating, and series arc-fault evaluations
show that the TinyML Smart Plug achieved the intended hybrid protection
behavior under controlled laboratory conditions. The calibrated voltage,
current, and temperature measurements were sufficient to support the protection
thresholds used in the prototype. The overload tests showed warning behavior
followed by relay trip only when sustained overload persisted, which is
consistent with the reviewed literature describing overload as a
time-dependent current and thermal-stress condition [12]-[14], [22]. The
socket-heating tests showed that degraded contact can produce faster and more
severe heating than current magnitude alone suggests, supporting the reviewed
finding that contact heating at the plug-socket interface can remain localized
and hidden from conventional upstream protection [17], [18], [24], [30], [31].
The arc-fault tests showed that waveform features and Random Forest inference
can support local relay action for series-arc events in the tested load set,
following the reviewed literature showing that series arcs may remain below
ordinary breaker trip levels while still producing waveform disturbance and
conduction intermittence [16], [33]-[39].

The feasibility of the prototype comes from matching each target fault
condition to the form of evidence that best represents it. Overload was treated
as a current-stress condition, so the protection response used threshold logic
with persistence. Contact heating was treated as a localized thermal condition
at the plug-socket interface, so the response used local temperature
supervision and compensated socket-temperature estimation. Series arcing was
treated as a load-dependent waveform condition, so the response used
multi-feature interpretation with load-family context rather than current
magnitude alone. This division follows the reviewed literature showing that
overload, contact heating, and series arcing are related electrical-fire
precursors, but they are not equivalent fault classes [34]-[39], [44]-[47].
The prototype therefore addressed the study objective by combining calibrated
sensing, threshold-based protection, load-family recognition, TinyML-supported
series-arc interpretation, and relay latching in one complementary socket-level
protection device.

The prototype remains a complementary protection device rather than a
replacement for circuit breakers, AFCIs, GFCIs, proper wiring practice,
contact inspection, or certified protection devices. This interpretation is
necessary because the evaluation was performed on a laboratory bench, the
thermal estimator was intentionally conservative, and the TinyML model was
trained and tested using a finite set of loads and induced arc conditions.
Within that boundary, the results show that the proposed smart plug can provide
earlier local warning, relay disconnection, and monitoring for selected
progressive fire-inducing electrical faults before they develop into more
severe electrical-fire events.

These findings support the main research objective by showing that the
developed TinyML Smart Plug can integrate calibrated voltage, current, and
temperature sensing with threshold-based protection, compensated
socket-temperature estimation, TinyML-supported series-arc interpretation,
relay actuation, local notification, and PWA monitoring. Through these
combined functions, the system responded to overload, socket heating, and series arc-fault events in a manner consistent with
its intended role as a complementary socket-level protection device for
progressive fire-inducing electrical faults.
