\section{Overload Protection}
\label{sec:ch4-overload-protection}

Five overload-related trials were conducted using high-power load combinations.
All trials exceeded the overload-warning threshold, but only the most severe
case remained above the sustained-overload criterion long enough to trip the
relay. This follows the firmware method in
\textit{Section~\ref{sec:ch3-overload-protection}}, where warning and sustained relay
trip are intentionally separated to distinguish short current excursions from
persistent thermal stress. This behavior is consistent with the reviewed
literature, which treats overload protection as a time-dependent overcurrent
condition rather than a purely instantaneous cutoff [22], [44], [45].

\textit{Table~\ref{tab:ch4-overload-evaluation}} shows that the prototype
issued overload warnings in all heavy-load trials, while relay disconnection
occurred only in Trial 4. Trial 4 had a mean current of 17.30A, which was
clearly above the 14.5A sustained-overload threshold. Its trip latency of 61.3s
also agrees with the intended approximately 60s persistence behavior of the
overload score. Trials 1, 3, and 5 reached peak currents near or above the
sustained-overload threshold, but their current levels did not remain above the
criterion long enough for the persistence score to reach the relay-trip value.

These results support the overload-protection objective because the prototype
operated as a warning-and-persistence device rather than as an instantaneous
current cutoff. The warning response gave early indication of heavy loading,
while the delayed relay trip was reserved for a sustained overcurrent condition.
Within the tested laboratory load combinations, this behavior reduced the chance
of tripping on short-duration peaks while still disconnecting the load when the
overload condition persisted.
